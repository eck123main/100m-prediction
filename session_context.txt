WHAT THE PROJECT DOES
Predicts men's 100m race win probabilities from ~5,356 scraped
worldathletics.org race results (2016-2026 dense coverage, scattered
championship finals back to 1983).

QUICK START — PREDICT A REAL RACE
  py -3.14 predict.py "Noah Lyles" "Kishane Thompson" "Fred Kerley" "Akani Simbine"
Prints win probabilities + weighted_avg_time, finish_rate, races_used, elo
per athlete. Case-insensitive name matching. Unmatched names are listed
separately, not silently dropped.

FEATURE PIPELINE (features.py)
1. load_clean_data() — loads CSV, normalizes names/dates, infers round from
   URL, filters implausible times (<9.0s or >13.0s — scraper bugs, see
   below), computes wind coefficient + round offsets + Elo history once.
2. Wind adjustment: estimate_wind_coefficient() — within-athlete fixed-
   effects regression (not a guessed physics constant). Came out to
   ~-0.04 to -0.05 sec per m/s, in line with published estimates.
3. Round adjustment: estimate_round_offsets() — same fixed-effects approach,
   normalizes heats/semis to a "final-equivalent" time. Heats ~+0.07s
   slower than final-equivalent, semis ~-0.02s faster (both small).
4. Reliability: compute_reliability_before() — Beta-shrunk finish rate
   (valid time vs DNF/DQ/DNS), leak-free (only uses races before cutoff).
5. Elo: compute_elo_history()/compute_elo_before() — sequential head-to-head
   rating from pairwise "who finished ahead of whom" in real single races
   only (get_real_races() filters field size 3-10 to exclude mislabeled
   multi-heat/full-meet dumps that show up under some source_urls).
6. compute_stats_before(cutoff_date, df) — the core leak-free join of all
   the above, using ONLY data strictly before cutoff_date. This is what
   makes backtesting honest.

PREDICTION FUNCTIONS (predict.py)
- predict_race() — naive baseline: rank by recency-weighted time only.
- simulate_race() — Monte Carlo: each athlete ~ Normal(weighted_avg_time,
  consistency), with a finish_rate coin-flip each simulated run (a DQ-prone
  athlete can simulate not finishing).
- elo_win_probs() — win probs from Elo alone (Bradley-Terry softmax).
- blend_win_probs() / predict_race_full() — RECOMMENDED. Blends Monte Carlo
  + Elo 50/50 — backtested better than either alone (see below).

BACKTEST RESULTS (backtest.py — run it for full breakdown incl. calibration
and miss-pattern analysis)
- 136 real finals evaluated (field size 3-10; source_urls with larger groups
  under "final" are mislabeled multi-heat/full-meet dumps, excluded).
- Monte Carlo alone: 41.2% top-1 accuracy vs 12.1% chance-level.
- Elo alone: 34.5% (weaker standalone signal than time-based model).
- Blended 50/50 (predict_race_full default): 42.6% overall, 46.8% excluding
  "cold start" races (winner had zero prior history — 8.8% of races,
  unpredictable by construction). elo_weight=0.5 beat 0.3 and 0.7 in every
  slice tested.
- Miss-pattern analysis: of 68 misses (excl. cold starts), actual winner was
  in the model's own top 2 half the time, top 3 two-thirds of the time —
  most misses are close calls, not wild misfires.
- Investigated "aging veteran overrated" hypothesis on 33 confidently-wrong
  misses (top_pick_prob >=0.40 but wrong): PARTIALLY true (Justin Gatlin
  clearly, Michael Rodgers more mixed) but NOT the dominant pattern — most
  confidently-wrong misses are between similarly-aged contemporaries with no
  age signal at all (e.g. Trayvon Bromell repeatedly over-favored at 26-27,
  losing to same-age or older rivals). Conclusion: ~42-47% top-1 is treated
  as close to a realistic ceiling for this data; building an age feature
  would need real birth-date data we don't currently scrape.

REAL BUGS FOUND AND FIXED THIS SESSION (not hypothetical — actually hit)
1. 14 rows had physically impossible 100m times (e.g. 45.12s for Christophe
   Lemaitre at Turku 2020) — scraper picked up the wrong row/column on
   specific pages. Filtered to NaN in load_clean_data (MIN/MAX_PLAUSIBLE_TIME
   = 9.0/13.0s). ROOT CAUSE IN SCRAPER NOT YET INVESTIGATED.
2. compute_stats_before crashed (KeyError) on cutoffs with zero prior
   history (the very first race(s) chronologically) — fixed by returning a
   properly-columned empty DataFrame instead of a columnless one.
3. Warning prints in predict.py crashed with UnicodeEncodeError on Windows
   (cp1252 console) for athlete names with accented characters — fixed by
   reconfiguring stdout to utf-8.
4. 66 of 203 raw "final"-round races actually have field sizes up to 125 —
   these are mislabeled multi-heat/full-meet dumps from calendar-results
   URLs where infer_round() can't detect the real round and defaults to
   "final". Worked around via MIN/MAX_REAL_RACE_SIZE (3-10) filtering in
   get_real_races() wherever pairwise/single-race logic matters (backtest,
   Elo) — NOT fixed at the scraper source.

KNOWN OPEN ITEMS / NOT YET BUILT
- No automated start-list scraping — upcoming race fields must be typed in
  by hand (CLI takes them as command-line args).
- Cold-start gap (~9% of winners had zero history) — more data collection
  would help, now with concrete backtest-driven justification (unlike
  earlier in the session, when we deliberately deferred blind collection).
- Scraper wrong-row bug (item 1 above) not root-caused, just filtered.
- Multi-heat "final" mislabeling (item 4 above) not fixed at the source.
- Elo weight (0.5) was chosen from only 3 candidates {0.3, 0.5, 0.7} tested
  on 136 races — a small sample; more rigorous tuning (e.g. train/test split
  across eras) wasn't done.

DATA QUALITY NOTES
- Dataset columns: ATHLETE, COUNTRY, time, record_flag, Reaction Time, wind,
  date, venue, meet_name, source_url, round.
- record_flag coverage ~7% (Q/q, PB/SB, NR/AR/WR/WL/OR/CR, DNS/DNF/DQ).
- wind coverage ~99%, date ~99%, venue ~90%, Reaction Time ~7% (mostly
  Olympics/Worlds pages that published it).
- ~1,677 unique athletes; median athlete has only 1 race; top athletes
  (Lyles, Bromell, Kerley, Bednarek, Hughes, Jacobs, Ujah, Baker) have
  13-37 races each spanning multiple years — this is where the model is
  most trustworthy.
